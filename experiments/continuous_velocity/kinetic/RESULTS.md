# Kinetic study results (campaign `kinetic_v1`, completed 2026-10-22)

Source of every number: `outputs/continuous_velocity/kinetic_v1/report/summary.json`
and `report/results.md`, regenerated by `report.py` from 742 complete evaluation
manifests (4,768 suite-sequences, zero training/evaluation source-hash
mismatches). Protocol: `outputs/continuous_velocity/kinetic_v1/protocol.json`
(version 2, written before any evaluation). Scores pool frames/pixels within
one fitted model and are reported as mean ± sample SD over the five fresh
training seeds 2027–2031; learning rates were selected per variant on seed 2026
by validation loss (every variant chose 1e-3). "Success" is schema-2 frame
localization success on visible frames. Group B models train on accelerated
streams and their speeds are not held out.

## Item 1: kinetic-world-models transport and refinement (Group A, constant-velocity training)

Core suite, held-out initial speeds .37/.63/1.23, horizon 40.

| Variant | Bilinear MSE | Bilinear success | Bilinear velocity error (px/frame) | Nearest MSE | Nearest success |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| base (parent physical model) | 0.001077 ± 0.000057 | 99.43% | 0.0060 | 0.0347 ± 0.0016 | 46.0% |
| alignS (spectral alignment) | 0.001110 ± 0.000093 | 99.41% | 0.0060 | 0.0354 | 44.8% |
| renderS (spectral rendering) | 0.001058 ± 0.000054 | 99.47% | 0.0059 | 0.0349 | 46.2% |
| spectral (both) | 0.001138 ± 0.000060 | 99.42% | 0.0060 | 0.0348 | 45.1% |
| **newton** (sub-pixel refinement) | **0.000535 ± 0.000066** | **99.88%** | **0.0039** | 0.0315 | 42.1% |
| spectral_newton | 0.000604 ± 0.000160 | 99.84% | 0.0043 | 0.0342 | 41.9% |

- The baseline reproduces the paper campaign (0.001094 there, four seeds).
- Spectral (Fourier-phase) alignment and rendering, the KWM transport, do not
  improve the trained model on either renderer. A no-learning oracle check during
  review favoured spectral transport with the parent's weights, but after
  training the bilinear decoder compensates and the difference vanishes. The
  transport is not where this model loses accuracy.
- Newton refinement of each pair displacement on the trigonometric interpolant
  of the masked similarity (KWM's phase-correlation refinement adapted to our
  masked similarity) halves the bilinear MSE, reduces the velocity error from
  0.0060 to 0.0039 px/frame and the internal transport error from 0.123 to
  0.080 px, at horizon 160 improves MSE from 0.00273 to 0.00160 and success from
  93.9% to 98.4%, and remains the best variant at 20k updates (seed 2026:
  spectral_newton 0.000295 vs base 0.000686 at horizon 40). It does not help
  nearest rendering (0.0315 vs 0.0347; success slightly lower), consistent with
  the review's prediction that nearest failure is an estimation problem the
  refinement cannot fix.
- Validation-only selection of the combined package chose `spectral_newton`
  (mean best-validation 0.00081 vs 0.00246 for base). On test it is not better
  than `newton` alone.
- Spectral transport is kept as an option. Both transports handle fractional
  (sub-pixel) displacements; the parent samples the memory bilinearly at the
  real-valued offset `v τ`, so sub-pixel motion was never the missing
  capability. What the Fourier shift removes is the interpolation blur of each
  transport, and that advantage is real but small: with the true velocity
  supplied to the same checkpoint, spectral transport gives 0.00012 vs 0.00015
  (bilinear), a difference the trained decoder absorbs. Its payoff appears in
  combination with the sharper estimate from Newton refinement under
  acceleration (`order2_spectral_newton`, below), where it also removes the
  order-2 cost on constant velocity.
- The roles split as follows: Newton refinement determines how precisely the
  motion is estimated; spectral transport determines how sharply an estimated
  motion is rendered; the two are independent switches and were tested both
  separately and together.

## Item 2: second-order (acceleration) motion

### Cost on constant-velocity data (Group A, core held-out trio, bilinear, horizon 40)

| Variant | MSE | Success | Horizon-160 MSE |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| base (order 1) | 0.001077 | 99.43% | 0.00273 |
| order2, κ=9 (gated, default) | 0.001370 ± 0.00019 | 99.20% | 0.00320 |
| order2, κ=4 | 0.001330 ± 0.00014 | 99.23% | — |
| order2, κ=0 (ungated) | 0.003549 ± 0.00013 | 95.30% | 0.0167 |
| order2_spectral_newton, κ=9 | 0.001056 ± 0.000028 | 99.24% | 0.00211 |

The evidence gate matters: without it, spurious curvature (amplified by τ²)
triples the MSE at horizon 40 and multiplies it by six at horizon 160. With the
gate the cost is about +0.0003 at horizon 40 (P3 fails its 1e-4 margin). The
zero-acceleration intervention on the trained order-2 model (`zeroacc_both`,
0.00134) is as bad as the model itself, so the residual cost is in the trained
features, not in test-time curvature. Combining order 2 with `spectral_newton`
removes the cost (0.001056, p=0.47 vs base; descriptive, the combination was
selected on validation only).

### Benefit under acceleration (accelerated suite, bilinear, horizon 40, 16 scenes × 3 initial speeds × 11 accelerations)

| Model | Training stream | MSE | Success | Transport error (px) |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A base (order 1) | constant velocity | 0.0249 ± 0.0002 | 56.7% | 2.92 |
| B base (order 1) | accelerated | 0.0205 ± 0.0006 | 57.7% | 2.90 |
| B relaxed (learned residual offsets) | accelerated | 0.0171 ± 0.0017 | 64.9% | 2.24 |
| B offset (free learned offsets) | accelerated | 0.0165 ± 0.0019 | 65.1% | 2.49 |
| A order2 κ=9 | constant velocity | 0.00472 ± 0.00035 | 92.2% | 0.53 |
| **B order2 κ=9** | accelerated | **0.00403 ± 0.00006** | **93.7%** | 0.48 |
| B order2 κ=4 | accelerated | 0.00318 ± 0.00012 | 95.6% | 0.39 |
| B order2 κ=0 | accelerated | 0.00273 ± 0.00010 | 96.7% | 0.37 |
| B order2_spectral_newton κ=9 | accelerated | 0.00179 ± 0.00025 | 98.4% | 0.26 |
| B order2 with (v, a) oracle | — | 0.00067 | 100% | ~0 |
| B order2, zero-acceleration intervention | — | 0.0235 | 57.9% | 2.86 |

By acceleration level (B order2 vs B base, bilinear success at horizon 40):
|a| = 0: 99.0% vs 98.3%; 5e-4: 98.5% vs 97.0%; 1e-3: 91.6% vs 86.2%;
2e-3: 87.4% vs 54.5%; 4e-3: 91.8% vs 29.6%; 1.2e-2: 96.5% vs 6.2%. The order-1
model's success collapses once |a| exceeds about 1e-3 px/frame² because it
estimates the mid-context velocity; the flexible learned-offset controls, which
receive a temporally resolved summary, recover only a fraction of the gap. The
zero-acceleration intervention removes the whole gain, so the benefit comes from
the acceleration term itself, not from different training. The gate costs
accuracy under acceleration (κ=0 is best there) and buys it on constant
velocity; κ=9 is the prespecified compromise. Horizon-80 results
(`accel_long`) show the same ordering (B order2 − B base: −0.0106; combined
variant −0.0203 vs base).

Nearest rendering remains unsolved by every variant (core nearest MSE ≈ 0.03
for all Group A models; accelerated nearest MSE ≈ 0.03–0.05), as the parent
study's handoff anticipated.

## What was not done

- No matched-trained FloWM or KWM baseline on this data; KWM's adaptive velocity
  nodes (its online recurrent estimator) were not ported, because the parent
  model already fits one continuous velocity from 36 pairs in batch mode.
- The frozen-FloWM channel densification with spectral transport was not
  implemented.
- Only horizontal motion was trained; the fit is two-dimensional but the 2-D
  suite of the parent study was not rerun.
- Gate calibration uses a prespecified 3σ rule whose nominal level is not
  achieved (measured null open rate about 1% on bilinear, 5% on nearest with
  parent-trained features); a frame-cluster variance estimator is future work.
